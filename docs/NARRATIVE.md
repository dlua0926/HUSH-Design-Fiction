# Narrative exploration — not final

## Intended dramatic structure
Exhausted allowance → sensitive need → decision whether to buy → meaningful consequence → buying later cannot undo past sharing → future privacy budgeting.

The public-facing ad may feel reassuring. The story should reveal the tensions of the system through everyday experience. Keep “Is this worth one Private Hour?” as a behavioural question rather than only a sales slogan.

| Unannounced relationship/travel search → parent alert | Withdrawn as presented | Arbitrary automatic family disclosure; unnecessary extra ecosystem assumptions |

| Course change / part-time shift allocation / mental-health support | Brainstorm options only | Not researched or selected; would need careful causal/institutional design |

The user gave a secret hotel stay/affair as an analogy for privacy needs and asked for a classroom-presentable equivalent. This did NOT select an affair as the final story.

## Selection checklist
For the final scene, explicitly answer:
1. What student-relevant activity needs privacy?
2. Who collects a signal, and what inference is made?
3. Who receives or uses it, through which specific service relationship?
4. What incentive explains that use?
5. What immediate change does the resident experience?
6. Which part would an active HUSH session prevent?
7. What remains after purchasing protection later?

Do not treat sharing Wi-Fi as permission for roommates/parents to see searches. Do not rely on “it's the future” to skip causal steps. Avoid inventing severe consequences simply to force a purchase. Keep uncertainty in inferred intent visible.

## Demonstration structure proposal
A continuous sequence with fewer speaker handoffs: household exhausted → resident's activity → consequence → purchase/limitation and closing. Speaker roles and exact duration are unassigned. An audience can explore both paid/unpaid paths after a rehearsed main demonstration.
